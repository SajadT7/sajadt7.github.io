Working Papers

  • Demographic and Labor Market Effects of ICE Detention Facility Expansion
    Working paper · November 2026
    [Latest Version]

    Abstract
    Using staggered ICE detention facility activations across U.S. counties from 2010 to 2023, I show that expanded detention capacity reduces Hispanic population, school enrollment, homeownership, and employment, with no comparable effects among non-Hispanic residents. Effects are concentrated near new facilities, where local ICE arrests also rise, suggesting that detention capacity facilitates enforcement and amplifies its chilling effects.

  • Universal FAFSA Mandates and the Activation of Student Aid
    with Toby Qin
    Applied Economics Letters · 2026
    [Journal]   [SSRN]

    Abstract
    Hard universal FAFSA mandates require high school seniors to complete the FAFSA, submit an alternative state aid form, or obtain a waiver before graduation. Using school-level Sun-Abraham event studies and state-year fixed-effects models for early mandates in Louisiana, Illinois, Alabama, and Texas, we find that mandates raise FAFSA completion by 12.3 percentage points and federal aid applicants by 0.087 per senior. State grant and scholarship aid rises modestly in later years, and enrollment effects are positive but statistically indistinguishable from zero.

  • Crime Risk, School Choice, and Enrollment: A Structural Analysis
    Work in progress · 2025

    Abstract
    This paper estimates a structural model of school choice that incorporates crime risk as a key determinant of parental decision-making. Using detailed data on Chicago public schools and geocoded crime incidents, the analysis examines how local crime exposure affects school choice probabilities and enrollment outcomes. The results highlight the role of neighborhood safety in shaping parental preferences and student sorting across schools.
