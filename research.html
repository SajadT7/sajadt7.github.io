Working Papers

  • Demographic and Labor Market Effects of ICE Detention Facility Expansion
    Working paper · November 2026
    [Latest Version]

    Abstract
    I study the demographic and labor market effects of ICE detention facilities using staggered facility activations across U.S. counties between 2010 and 2023. Facility activation reduces Hispanic population, public school enrollment, homeownership, and employment, with no comparable effects among non-Hispanic residents. Declines are concentrated near newly activated facilities, consistent with heightened perceptions of enforcement risk. Local ICE arrests also increase around recent facility activations, suggesting that expanded detention capacity facilitates local enforcement. These findings highlight the role of detention infrastructure in shaping the chilling effects of immigration enforcement.

  • Universal FAFSA Mandates and the Activation of Student Aid
    with Toby Qin
    Applied Economics Letters · 2026
    [Journal]   [SSRN]

    Abstract
    Hard universal FAFSA mandates require high school seniors to complete the FAFSA, submit an alternative state aid form, or obtain a waiver before graduation. We study early graduation-linked mandates in Louisiana, Illinois, Alabama, and Texas, building on prior state-specific causal evidence. We estimate completion effects with school-level Sun-Abraham event studies and downstream responses with state-year fixed-effects models. Our estimates indicate that hard mandates increase FAFSA completion by 12.3 percentage points. Federal student aid applicants rise by 0.087 applicants per senior. In later post years, state grant and scholarship aid rises by 3.5 percentage points of average public in-state tuition and fees. Enrollment estimates are positive but statistically indistinguishable from zero. The results suggest that hard mandates generate a strong filing first stage, but the associated rise in grant and scholarship aid may be too modest to incentivize additional enrollment effects.

  • Crime Risk, School Choice, and Enrollment: A Structural Analysis
    Work in progress · 2025

    Abstract
    This paper estimates a structural model of school choice that incorporates crime risk as a key determinant of parental decision-making. Using detailed data on Chicago public schools and geocoded crime incidents, the analysis examines how local crime exposure affects school choice probabilities and enrollment outcomes. The results highlight the role of neighborhood safety in shaping parental preferences and student sorting across schools.
