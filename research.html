Work in Progress

  • "Universal FAFSA Mandates and the Activation of Student Aid"
    with Toby Qin
    Accepted for Publication at Applied Economics Letters, 2026
    [SSRN]
    Abstract
    Hard universal FAFSA mandates require high school seniors to complete the FAFSA, submit an alternative state aid form, or obtain a waiver before graduation. We study early graduation-linked mandates in Louisiana, Illinois, Alabama, and Texas, building on prior state-specific causal evidence. We estimate completion effects with school-level Sun-Abraham event studies and downstream responses with state-year fixed-effects models. Our estimates indicate that hard mandates increase FAFSA completion by 12.3 percentage points. Federal student aid applicants rise by 0.087 applicants per senior. In later post years, state grant and scholarship aid rises by 3.5 percentage points of average public in-state tuition and required fees. Enrollment estimates are positive but statistically indistinguishable from zero. The results suggest that hard mandates generate a strong filing first stage, but the associated rise in grant and scholarship aid may be too modest to incentivize additional enrollment effects.
  • "Demographic and Labor Market Effects of ICE Detention Facility Expansion"
    2025
    Abstract
    This paper studies the demographic and labor market effects of ICE detention facilities. We exploit the staggered activation of facilities across counties between 2010 and 2023 within a difference-in-differences framework. Facility activation reduces the Hispanic population, with corresponding declines in Hispanic public school enrollment, homeownership, and employment. Non-Hispanic outcomes show no comparable response. Point estimates are larger in counties with greater detention intensity and larger baseline Hispanic population shares, though the subgroup differences are not statistically distinguishable. A block-group analysis of areas near activated facilities corroborates the county-level results, with effects concentrated in the block groups closest to activated facilities providing evidence for salient mechanism. County-level arrest records show that ICE arrests increase around recent facility activations, providing suggestive evidence consistent with an enforcement-capacity mechanism. These findings complement a growing literature on the chilling effects of immigration enforcement on communities by examining detention infrastructure as a supply-side shock to enforcement capacity.
  • "Crime Risk, School Choice, and Enrollment: A Structural Analysis"
    2025
    Abstract
    This paper estimates a structural model of school choice that incorporates crime risk as a key determinant of parental decision-making. Using detailed data on Chicago public schools and geocoded crime incidents, the analysis examines how local crime exposure affects school choice probabilities and enrollment outcomes. The results highlight the role of neighborhood safety in shaping parental preferences and student sorting across schools.
